An ectopic kidney that was not the problem

The scare. Abdominal pain. The ultrasound shows mesenteric lymph nodes and an ectopic kidney. The parents worry about the kidney.
The cause. The lymph nodes explain the pain. The kidney is incidental.
The quick check. Identifiers removed, ChatGPT is asked: with normal vitals and urine, is more evaluation needed now?
The answer. For this well child, with another cause for the pain: no further tests now. Keep monitoring.
The second source. A trusted website, not another chatbot: the National Kidney Foundation says an ectopic kidney needs treatment only if it affects urine flow.
About 2.5 minutes. Click through the five steps; the arrow keys step through them too. The report on screen is a dummy, not the real one. The message: the parents were frightened by a finding that had nothing to do with the pain, and a quick check with ChatGPT helped you frame calm, clear counselling. Then the most important step: you did not stop at the chatbot. Most of us in UP cannot ring a pediatric nephrologist, so you checked a trusted website, here the National Kidney Foundation. Say it plainly: confirm with a second source, never with another chatbot. Be ready for the question every pediatrician will ask: what about reflux or a urine infection? What this means

ChatGPT picks the most likely next word, again and again. The result reads smoothly whether it is right or wrong.

Fluent is not the same as checked.

About 1.5 minutes. Keep this simple and non-technical. Click to show the predicted next words. Say: it is a very good autocomplete. It has read a great deal, so it is often right, but it has no built-in way of knowing when it is wrong. That is why it can write a confident reference that does not exist. This sets up the spot-the-error exercise later.
